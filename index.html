INTRODUCTION AND PLACE PROFILE

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 03

Chhattisgarh is a state in central India, formed on 1 November 2000 after separation from Madhya Pradesh.

The capital is ; the state is known for forests, mineral resources, agriculture, tribal culture, and industry.

The ancient region associated with present-day Chhattisgarh is commonly known as Dakshin Kosala.

The state combines rural and urban communities, traditional livelihoods, and modern infrastructure development.

GEOGRAPHICAL DETAILS AND POPULATION

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 04

Located in central India; it shares boundaries with Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana, Odisha, Jharkhand, and Uttar Pradesh.

Terrain includes plains, plateaus, forested hills, river valleys, and mineral-rich regions.

The Mahanadi and its tributaries are important to the state's geography and agriculture.

The 2011 Census recorded a population of approximately 2.55 crore; newer estimates may differ.

ENVIRONMENTAL DETAILS

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 05

Chhattisgarh has extensive forests, diverse wildlife, rivers, waterfalls, and mineral deposits.

Forests support biodiversity and many rural and tribal livelihoods.

Concerns include forest degradation, mining and industrial pollution, water pressure, and waste management.

Useful measures: native-species afforestation, water conservation, cleaner industry, renewable energy, responsible mining, and recycling.

HABITAT AND LIFESTYLE

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 06

People live in villages, small towns, and growing urban centres such as , Bilaspur, Durg-Bhilai, and Korba.

Rural livelihoods include farming, forest-produce collection, animal husbandry, crafts, and local trade.

Culture is expressed through folk music and dance, festivals, traditional crafts, cuisine, and regional languages.

Access to education, healthcare, transport, and commerce varies; remote forested areas may face longer travel distances.

: Indravati River basin, Bastar (~300 m monsoon crest)

TECHNOLOGY AND SCIENCE

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 07

Technology supports mining, steel production, power generation, agriculture, transport, public services, and urban management.

Digital services and mobile connectivity can improve access to information and government services.

Potential improvements include sensor-based mine safety and predictive maintenance in factories.

Solar-powered cold storage, digital marketplaces, offline-first healthcare tools, and regional-language STEM platforms can help.

Innovation should be affordable, accessible to rural users, locally suitable, and supported by training and maintenance.

AMENITIES AVAILABLE

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 08

Education: schools, colleges, technical institutes, universities, and skill-development centres operate across the state.

Healthcare: government hospitals, community health centres, primary health centres, and frontline health workers provide services.

Transport and utilities: roads, railways, electricity, mobile networks, and water-supply systems support communities and industry.

Urban services: major cities provide markets, public facilities, waste-management services, and selected digital and smart-city systems.

Access and quality can vary by region, with specialist healthcare and utilities harder to reach in some remote areas.

CHALLENGES

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 09

Uneven access to healthcare and education, rural connectivity gaps, post-harvest losses, and limited cold-chain storage.

Market-access barriers for local producers and artisans.

Mining and industrial activity can cause air and water pollution, land disturbance, and worker-safety risks.

Infrastructure projects must consider forests, wildlife, and local communities.

Ensure investment, technology, and skill-development benefits reach small businesses, rural residents, and tribal communities fairly.

SUGGESTIONS / MODIFICATIONS

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 10

Set up affordable solar-powered cold rooms and shared processing units near farming and forest-produce clusters.

Improve mine and factory safety with sensors, worker alerts, preventive maintenance, and regular training.

Expand telemedicine, offline-capable health tools, and reliable emergency transport for remote settlements.

Support tribal artisans through fair-price digital marketplaces, product photography, packaging, and direct buyer links.

Provide portable science kits, maker spaces, regional-language learning resources, and industry mentorship.

Encourage cleaner production, wastewater treatment, recycling, renewable energy, and restoration of mined land.

Consult local residents and monitor accessibility, environmental effects, and fair distribution of benefits.

GOVERNMENT FACILITIES AND SCHEMES

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 11

Residents may qualify for programmes related to food security, rural employment, housing, health coverage, farmer support, scholarships, skills, and tribal welfare.

Examples include MGNREGA, PMAY (housing), Ayushman Bharat / PM-JAY (eligible health coverage), and PM-KISAN (eligible farmers).

Scholarship schemes may support eligible students.

Availability and eligibility rules vary; confirm current details with official state portals, relevant departments, or local government service centres.

CONCLUSION

Chhattisgarh has strengths in agriculture, forests, minerals, energy, industry, and cultural heritage.

These resources create opportunities for inclusive economic and social development.

Future progress can be strengthened through practical innovation, better public amenities, environmental protection, quality education, and healthcare access.

Fair opportunities for rural and tribal communities are essential for balanced development.

SCOPE AND FIELD QUESTIONS

What this survey establishes — and what it does not

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 13

This presentation combines a statewide background survey with dated government indicators. The figures describe the state; they do not describe every village.

Evidence boundary: The project folder contains no household interviews, village service measurements, or team field photographs. No primary field results are claimed here.

Questions for immersion: How long does it take to reach a health centre, school, market, and bus stop? Which services work reliably? Which livelihoods change by season?

Record with consent: location and date, travel time, service availability, seasonal changes, and residents’ priorities. Separate observed facts from reported experiences.

FUTURE WORK & INNOVATION ROADMAP

Proposed field immersion methodologies, technical interventions, and academic milestones

CHHATTISGARH FORMAL SURVEY REPORT • 16

Pillar 01 • Grassroots Fieldwork

Primary Gram Panchayat Immersion Survey

  • Conduct structured community surveys across targeted Gram Panchayats in Bastar, Kondagaon, and Mahasamund.
  • Administer semi-structured interviews with Self-Help Groups (SHGs), Primary Health Centres (PHCs), and local Panchayati Raj representatives.
  • Document indigenous farming knowledge and traditional water harvesting systems.
Methodology: Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA)
Pillar 02 • Technical Innovation

Offline-First Mobile Survey Tooling

  • Engineer an open-source, offline-first Progressive Web App (PWA) with SQLite local caching for zero-connectivity forest zones.
  • Integrate bilingual Chhattisgarhi/Hindi audio questions for intuitive survey completion by non-literate community members.
  • Implement GPS timestamp verification for transparent field survey logging.
Tech Stack: PWA · IndexedDB · OpenStreetMap
Pillar 03 • Livelihood Systems

Agro-Forestry Value Chain Interventions

  • Analyze processing, storage, and procurement bottlenecks for Minor Forest Produce (Mahua, Tendu Patta, Sal Seed, Harra).
  • Examine market linkages and fair-trade direct aggregation models for Bastar Dokra metalcraft and Kosa silk artisans.
  • Evaluate solar mini-cold storage viability for perishable tribal produce.
Focus: Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs)
Pillar 04 • Policy & Impact

GIS Spatial Dashboard & Open Academic Repository

  • Synthesize village-level amenity access indicators into an interactive GIS map layer for public review.
  • Formulate actionable policy briefs and village-specific suggestions for rural development stakeholders and district administration.
  • Publish anonymized survey datasets in an institutional open-access repository.
Deliverable: Interactive Spatial Decision Dashboard
